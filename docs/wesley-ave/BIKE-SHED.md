# A bike shed for six XL mountain bikes at 2926 Wesley Ave

9 November 2026. Options thought through with MK, the chosen design worked up as a framed
SketchUp model he can build from. This is MK's Charlotte house, not 146 Mick Road, so nothing
here touches the HomePlan app or its plans; the photos and plat are in [photos/](photos/).

## Update, 9 November, later: roof flipped toward the wall, covering the strip behind

MK asked to pitch the roof toward the retaining wall rather than the yard, with no need for a
steep roof (Charlotte sees little snow), and to carry it over the 3' gap behind the shed so that
strip becomes covered storage for wheelsets and long tools. He also noted he has only four to six
wheels, so the big wheel shelf is not needed. Worked through and agreed:

- **Heights.** Flipping makes the door wall the tall one (122") and the rack wall the low one.
  The racks need about 102" clear (high cradle plus bracket), so the back wall stops at 106",
  which caps the pitch at 2:12 under the 12' height rule. The 3.35:12 could not flip without
  losing the high rack row.
- **Roofing at 2:12.** Exposed-fastener panels are rated 3:12 without sealant; the IBC and the
  Texas evaluation reports for Classic Rib allow down to 1/2:12 with butyl tape at every side and
  end lap and stitch screws at 12" o.c., over a synthetic underlayment. Charlotte's issue is 43"
  of rain, not snow, so the lap sealing is the whole job.
- **The cantilever.** The 2x6 rafters run 26" past the back wall, leaving the roof edge 10" short
  of the retaining wall face: a permit-exempt shed may sit near the line but its projections may
  not cross it, and the wall is on the right-of-way line. A 26" cantilever on the 92.5" back span
  is well within a 2x6 at 24" o.c. and puts no uplift on the front wall. Rafters become 2x6 x 12'.
  The roof underside is 9'2" above the pad at the edge, so long tools stand upright.
- **Permit.** The NC exemption is written on floor area ("does not exceed 12 feet in any
  dimension"), so eaves do not count; the floor stays 12' x 8'. Roof top about 141" above the
  pad at the front lip of the metal, 3" under the 12' line.

## What six XL bikes need

| Dimension | Used here | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Overall length | 80" (allow 82") | Honzo ST XL wheelbase 1248 mm per Kona; long enduro bikes to 1300 mm; 29 x 2.5 tyre about 750 mm |
| Handlebar width | 32" | 800 mm bars |
| Standing height to bar top | 44 to 46" | riser bar, XL stack |
| Weight | 30 to 36 lb; e-MTB 50 lb or more | |

**Hung vertically on one wall** (Steadyrack or hooks, every other bike higher):

- 16" centres. Steadyrack's staggered figure for 800 mm bars is 400 mm, 600 mm if not
  staggered. The end bikes need about 16" to the side wall, so six bikes take
  16 + 5 x 16 + 16 = 112" of wall, which is why the shed is 10' long. Seven fit at 14" only
  with narrow bars.
- Low racks top out at about 84" above the floor (bike length plus wheel clearance), the
  high ones at 96". The hanging wall must be at least 8' inside; this design gives 9'6".
- A bike hung straight out projects its standing height, about 44", from the wall. Swung
  sideways on a Steadyrack it projects about 22". Loading needs about 5' of clear floor in
  front of the rack for a moment.
- Steadyracks rather than plain hooks for 35 lb XL bikes: you lift only the front wheel into
  the cradle and roll the bike up; the rear wheel stays on the floor until the bike is
  vertical. The eBike model is rated to 35 kg (77 lb).

**Parked on the floor, nose in, alternating front-wheel height** (the bike-parking way) takes
the same 16" bays and 112" width, 84" of depth and only a 7' ceiling, but every bay must be
reached from a door on the long side, so the front needs about 8' of opening, and nothing else
fits in the room. Both methods land on the same 10' x 7' footprint.

## Options

**A. Stretch the existing shed forward 6'.** Take the door wall off, extend skids, both side
walls and the roof plane 6' toward the yard, put the door wall back on the new front; bikes on
the tall fence-side wall in the front 10', mower and tools in the back 6'. One building,
128 sq ft, no new roof to design, the cheapest way to a tall 10' wall. Against: it eats 6' of
the turf (about 30' deep between shed and house by the plat), the bikes share a room with
gas and the mower, and the tool chest and shelving on that wall have to move to the back.

**B. A twin shed beside the existing one.** A second 8' x 10' lean-to style shed on the
window side, same slope and look. It does not fit: the strip beside the shed is a wedge that
starts at nothing, so a building parallel to the existing shed has to be pushed well out into
the yard before 8' of width clears the wall.

**C. A dedicated bike shed in the wedge, parallel to the retaining wall.** 10' x 7', the
tall wall at the back carrying six Steadyracks, the roof draining to the yard, a 5' double door
on the yard side. 70 sq ft, bikes away from fuel and dirt, the siding and roof matched to the
existing shed so the two read as a pair. It needs about 11' of clear wall length from the
sawhorse corner and about 8' of wedge depth where it sits. **Chosen.**

**D. Racks in the existing shed plus a mower lean-to.** Six Steadyracks on the existing tall
wall now (the tool chest rolls to the back), the bench stays on the low side, and a 4' x 10'
lean-to under the low eave takes mower, blower, gas cans and garden tools. Lowest cost and a
good test of vertical hanging, but the shed stays a shared, crowded room, the 7'5" interior is
marginal for loading racks with a bench opposite, and the shelving still needs a home.

**Dismissed.** A lean-to addition on the existing shed's window side: that is its 7' low side,
so a roof continuing the slope ends under 5' tall at 7' out, and a reversed slope makes a
valley at the joint. Additions on the fence side or the back: no room. Tear down and rebuild
bigger: only worth it if the shed were failing, and it looks sound.

## Permits and zoning

- North Carolina exempts one-storey detached accessory buildings with no dimension over 12'
  from a building permit (the inspections pages for Wake, Johnston and Brunswick counties all
  state the 12' rule; Mecklenburg applies the same state code). This shed is 10' long, 7' deep
  and 11'3" tall, so it qualifies. Running power to it needs an electrical permit.
- Charlotte's Unified Development Ordinance still governs placement. Summaries give 3' or 5'
  from side and rear lot lines for accessory structures, not in a front or street-side setback.
  The catch here is that the Matheson side is a street right of way, not a neighbour's rear
  line, so the UDO may treat it as a street side. The existing shed already sits within a few
  feet of it. Confirm with Charlotte Planning which setback applies at the retaining wall
  before fixing the gap; the model carries 36".
- Lot coverage is not a concern: the two sheds together are about 150 sq ft on a 7,840 sq ft
  lot.
