# 146 Mick Road: the stove's flue outside the house (v7)

## Context

MK's brief of 14 Oct 2026: route the new wood stove's chimney outside the house, in compliance with
the New York building code. In PLAN-v6 it went straight up from the stove through the bedroom
upstairs (in a 24 x 20 chase) and out through the body of the main roof near the lake eave.

The stove stays where PLAN-v6 put it (the Living's south-west corner, its back to the west wall).
The west wall behind it is inside the proposed screen porch (x -96..0, y 158..315) and the lake
window starts 8 1/2" from the corner on the south wall, so the only way out is west, into the
porch's south-east corner.

## The code

The house is a one-family dwelling in New York State outside New York City (assumed: the city has
its own code). The governing code is the **2025 Residential Code of New York State** (part of the
2025 Uniform Code, based on the 2024 IRC), in force since 31 Dec 2025. The section numbers below are
the IRC's. The code sites could not be reached from here, so the 2025 RCNYS text itself was not
read: check the numbers and any New York amendments in it before the permit application.

## The route (inches; x east, y south; elevations absolute, the First Floor's top is 2)

- **Connector** (inside): a 6" double-wall black stovepipe, 8" outside, from the stove's collar at
  (19, 280), 33, straight up to an elbow at 72, then west into the **wall thimble** centred 66"
  over the floor (68), above the 60" stone surround. Drawn as an uncapped flue column in the
  stove's black and a black box (`Stovepipe`) from the wall's inside face to the column.
- **Chimney** (outside): an insulated 6" stainless chimney, 8" outside, at (-6, 280), 2" clear of
  the siding (x -10..-2), from its **tee** and cleanout (58; the tee's branch is the `Tee` box in
  the screen porch, x -6..0) straight up to 310, with a rain cap. It passes through the screen
  porch's shed roof 6" from the house wall (its top about 130.5 there), runs up the gable wall, and
  passes through the main roof's 24" **rake overhang** (its top 226 there). The roof 10' up the
  slope is at 286.

## Left to the installer and the code official

- **The stove and the chimney maker** decide the numbers: their instructions set the clearances,
  the thimble, the supports, the flashings, the cap and the termination, and win over the plan.
- **Roof brace**: the chimney stands 7' over the rake. Above 5' the makers call for a roof brace
  (DuraVent's DuraTech manual, for one).
- **Porch roof flashing**: the pipe passes 6" from the house wall, where the roof meets it. The
  flashing has to be worked into the wall's step and counter flashing, or the pipe held further
  off the wall.
- **Firestops and shields** where the chimney passes the rake's soffit and the porch roof's
  framing, per the listing; **wall bands** up the gable wall at the listing's spacing.
- In the porch the pipe and its tee are at hand height and warm. A guard is an option, not a code
  item.
- An outside chimney is colder than an inside one and drafts less well at start-up. The insulated
  chimney helps. This is performance, not code.
- A building permit and inspections from the town's code enforcement officer.
